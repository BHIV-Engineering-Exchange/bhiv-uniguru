# Yantra

## Canonical Name

Yantra

## Sanskrit

यन्त्र

## Transliteration

Yantra

## Śabda

यन्त्र

## Dhātu

यम् (Yam) — To restrain, to hold, to support, to govern.

## Vyākaraṇa

Derived from the verbal root यम् (yam) with the suffix -tra (instrument suffix). Neuter noun. Pāṇini Ashtadhyayi 3.2.182 governs the -tra suffix. Yantra = that which restrains, holds, or governs; an instrument of control and support.

## Nirukta

"Yantrayati iti yantraḥ" — that which restrains or governs. The root yam carries the sense of holding in place, governing, and directing. A yantra holds the divine presence in geometric form.

## Bīja

The bīja of the specific deity whose yantra is being used. For Śrī Yantra: hrīṃ (Māyā-śakti bīja). For Gaṇeśa Yantra: gaṃ. For Sūrya Yantra: hrāṃ. The central bindu (point) of every yantra is itself the supreme bīja.

## Tattva

Yantra-Tattva — the principle of geometric form as a vehicle of divine presence. In Tantric philosophy: the yantra is the geometric body of the deity, as the mantra is the sonic body and the mūrti (image) is the physical body. The yantra operates through the principle of correspondence between geometric form and cosmic structure.

## Śakti

Yantra-Śakti — the power concentrated in geometric form. The Śrī Yantra is considered the geometric form of Śakti herself. The nine interlocking triangles of the Śrī Yantra represent the nine forms of Śakti. The bindu at the center is the seat of Śakti's supreme power.

## Literal Meaning

Instrument; device; that which restrains or holds; a geometric diagram.

## Functional Meaning

Yantra is a geometric diagram used as a support for meditation, worship, and the concentration of divine energy. It is the visual counterpart of mantra — as mantra is the sonic form of the deity, yantra is the geometric form. The yantra operates through the principle that specific geometric configurations correspond to specific states of consciousness and cosmic forces. The practitioner meditates on the yantra, moving from the outer geometric forms inward to the central bindu, which represents the undifferentiated source.

## Ontology

In Tantric philosophy: the yantra is not merely a symbol but a living geometric body of the deity. The Śrī Yantra is the geometric form of the entire cosmos as Śakti's body. In Advaita: the yantra is an upāsanā (contemplative) support that leads the mind from form to formlessness. In Śākta tradition: the yantra is the primary form of the Goddess — more fundamental than the anthropomorphic image.

## Cosmology

The Śrī Yantra represents the entire cosmos: the nine interlocking triangles represent the nine levels of manifestation. The outer square represents the earth plane. The lotus petals represent the unfolding of creation. The central bindu represents the undifferentiated source. The yantra is a map of the cosmos and a map of consciousness simultaneously.

## Psychology

The yantra functions as a visual mantra — sustained meditation on the yantra produces the same psychological transformation as mantra recitation. The geometric progression from outer to inner mirrors the psychological journey from gross to subtle consciousness. The central bindu represents the state of pure awareness beyond all form.

## Governance

In Vedic governance: yantras were used in the consecration of temples, cities, and royal palaces. The concept of vāstu-yantra (architectural yantra) governs the geometric layout of sacred spaces. The Arthaśāstra describes the use of protective yantras in statecraft.

## Medicine

In Āyurveda: yantras are used as protective amulets (rakṣā-yantra) and healing supports. Specific yantras are prescribed for specific conditions. The Mahāmṛtyuñjaya Yantra is used for healing and protection.

## Mathematics

The Śrī Yantra is a mathematical marvel: the nine interlocking triangles create 43 smaller triangles with precise geometric relationships. The angles and proportions of the Śrī Yantra follow specific mathematical rules. The concept of sacred geometry (yantra-gaṇita) is a distinct mathematical tradition.

## Astronomy

In Jyotiṣa: each of the nine planets (navagraha) has a corresponding yantra. Planetary yantras are used during specific astronomical configurations. The Navagraha Yantra maps the nine planets in a geometric arrangement.

## Metallurgy

Yantras are traditionally inscribed on metal plates (copper, gold, silver) or stone. The choice of metal corresponds to the deity: gold for solar deities, silver for lunar deities, copper for Śakti. The process of yantra inscription involves specific mantras and ritual procedures.

## Ritual

The yantra is the primary ritual support in Tantric worship (pūjā). The ritual of yantra-prāṇa-pratiṣṭhā (consecration of the yantra) installs the deity's presence in the geometric form. The Navāvaraṇa pūjā (nine-enclosure worship) of the Śrī Yantra is the most elaborate Tantric ritual.

## Symbolism

The triangle pointing upward (trikona) represents Śiva/masculine principle. The triangle pointing downward represents Śakti/feminine principle. The interlocking triangles represent the union of Śiva and Śakti. The lotus represents purity and unfolding. The square represents the earth and the four directions. The bindu represents the undifferentiated source.

## Traditional Interpretations

- Śākta: The yantra is the primary form of the Goddess — more fundamental than the anthropomorphic image; the Śrī Yantra is the Goddess herself
- Kashmir Śaivism: The yantra is the geometric form of Śiva's consciousness; meditation on the yantra is meditation on consciousness itself
- Advaita: The yantra is an upāsanā support that leads the mind from form to formlessness
- Vaiṣṇava: Yantras are used in temple worship as geometric supports for deity presence
- Smārta: Yantras are used in the pañcāyatana (five-deity) worship system

## Historical Evolution

- Vedic period: Geometric altars (vedi) in Śulbasūtras are proto-yantras
- Upaniṣadic period: The concept of the body as yantra (the body as the deity's geometric form)
- Tantric period (500–1200 CE): Yantras systematized; the Śrī Yantra reaches its classical form
- Medieval: Yantra traditions codified in Āgamas and Tantras
- Modern: Yantras used in meditation and as sacred art
